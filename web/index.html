RULES OF THE FOG

Tic-tac-toe, except knowledge is a resource.

Three in a row still wins. The twist is what each player is allowed to know.

01

Choose the mystery cells

Before the match, select at least two cells to live inside the fog. Both players know which locations are mysterious.

02

Visible moves are public

Play on a normal cell and both players see the mark. It behaves exactly like ordinary tic-tac-toe.

03

Fog moves are private

Attempt a mystery cell and the opponent only learns that you played somewhere in the fog—not which cell.

04

First claim wins the cell

If the mystery cell is unclaimed, your mark owns it. If the opponent already claimed it, your attempt consumes the turn and the original mark remains.

05

No repeated probing

Each player may attempt a particular mystery cell only once. Your own previous attempts are known to you.

06

Three in a row wins

The real underlying board decides the result. Hidden ownership counts toward a line even when the other player cannot see it.

WHAT “OPTIMAL” MEANS HERE

One current action for an entire information set.

The solver applies the same candidate move to every state the acting player considers possible, treats those states as equally likely, and averages their continuation values.

Q(I, a) = 1 / |I| · Σ V(T(s, a))

Important: because the solver does not know the opponent’s private information set, future value currently uses perfect-information minimax as an approximation. So “Optimal” means optimal under this current-action belief model, not a proven extensive-form equilibrium.
