RULES

Tic-tac-toe with hidden ownership.

The board locations that are mysterious are public. Their ownership—and the result of a mystery attempt—may not be.

01

Make three in a row

O and X alternate turns. The first player with three owned cells in a row, column, or diagonal wins.

02

Mystery locations are public

Before play, both players know which board locations are mystery cells. What can remain hidden is who actually owns them.

03

Visible cells are ordinary

Claim an empty visible cell normally. The move and location are public, and an occupied visible cell cannot be chosen.

04

A fog action hides its location

When a player acts on a mystery cell, the opponent learns only that a fog action happened—not which mystery cell was attempted.

05

A mystery attempt can fail silently

If the cell is empty, the acting player claims it. If the opponent owns it, the attempt fails and still consumes the turn. The acting player receives no direct success/failure signal.

06

You may probe each mystery cell once

Each player may attempt a particular mystery cell at most once. Players remember which mystery locations they personally attempted.

07

The true board decides the game

Victory is checked on the underlying board after each successful claim. When the game ends, the true hidden board is revealed.
