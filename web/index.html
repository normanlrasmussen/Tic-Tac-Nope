HOW TO PLAY

The rules of Tic-Tac-Nope.

It is tic-tac-toe with hidden ownership on designated mystery cells. The true board decides the game; players only act on what they have observed.

01

Make three in a row

O and X alternate turns. As in ordinary tic-tac-toe, the first player with three owned cells in a row, column, or diagonal wins.

02

Some cells are mysterious

Before play begins, certain board locations are designated as mystery cells and both players know which locations those are. What is hidden is who actually owns them during play.

03

Normal cells behave normally

On a visible cell, a player may claim an empty location. The move and location are public, and an already occupied visible cell cannot be chosen.

04

A mystery move hides its location

When a player acts on a mystery cell, the opponent learns only that a fog action occurred. The opponent is not told which mystery cell was attempted.

05

Mystery attempts can fail silently

If the attempted mystery cell is empty, the acting player claims it. If the opponent already owns it, the attempt fails and still consumes the turn. The acting player receives no direct success-or-failure signal; ownership is known only when it can be inferred from the observations available to that player.

06

You may probe each mystery cell once

Each player may attempt a particular mystery cell at most once. Players remember which mystery cells they personally attempted, but they do not receive or remember a separate success-or-failure result.

07

The true board ends the game

After every successful claim, victory is checked on the underlying board—including hidden ownership. If the board fills with no three-in-a-row, the game is a draw. At the end, the true board is revealed.
